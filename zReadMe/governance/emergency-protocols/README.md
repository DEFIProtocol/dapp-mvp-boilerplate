# DCSN Emergency Protocols

This document operationalizes Article VII of the DAO Constitution.

---

## 1. Emergency Conditions

Emergency powers may be considered when one or more of the following occur:

- regional or global supply chain collapse  
- severe regional instability or conflict  
- natural disaster impacting critical infrastructure  
- systemic failure of traditional financial or logistics systems  
- critical backlog of essential goods (food, fuel, key resources)  

---

## 2. Emergency Proposal Requirements

Emergency proposals must include:

- clear description of the emergency condition  
- affected regions and commodities  
- proposed actions (incentive changes, routing rules, subsidies, etc.)  
- expected duration  
- risks and potential unintended consequences  
- rollback conditions  

Where possible, emergency proposals should be narrowly scoped and time‑bound.

---

## 3. Available Emergency Actions

The DAO may:

- **Increase incentives**  
  - higher rewards for specific routes, roles, or commodities  

- **Reroute deliveries**  
  - prioritize alternative paths or nodes  
  - temporarily adjust routing logic  

- **Subsidize transport**  
  - cover part of fuel, tolls, or operational costs  

- **Prioritize essential commodities**  
  - food  
  - fuel  
  - key agricultural commodities  
  - critical raw materials  

- **Activate special protocols**  
  - fast‑track verification  
  - temporary relaxation of non‑critical constraints (never safety)  

---

## 4. Time Limits and Renewal

- Emergency powers **expire automatically after 14 days** unless renewed by a new vote.  
- Renewal requires:
  - updated justification  
  - impact assessment (if available)  
  - confirmation that conditions still warrant emergency status  

---

## 5. Safeguards

To prevent abuse:

- All emergency proposals and votes are public and immutable.  
- Emergency actions must not:
  - centralize control  
  - permanently alter core governance guarantees  
  - violate censorship resistance or neutrality  

- After an emergency period, a post‑incident review is recommended:
  - what worked  
  - what failed  
  - what should be codified or changed for future events
