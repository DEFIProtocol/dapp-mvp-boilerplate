⭐ SUPPLY‑CHAIN COMPETENCY TEST (Simple, Teach‑as‑You‑Go Version)
Multiple‑choice included, but each option teaches something.
1. A factory makes 1,000 units a day. Suddenly demand jumps to 2,000. What happens first?
A) Prices rise because supply can’t keep up

B) Prices fall because people want more

C) Nothing changes

D) The factory instantly doubles production

Correct answer: A  
Teaches: supply shortage → price signal.

2. A trucking company has fewer drivers than usual. What is the most likely effect on the supply chain?
A) Deliveries slow down and costs rise

B) Deliveries speed up

C) Prices drop because trucks are empty

D) Nothing changes

Correct answer: A  
Teaches: labor bottlenecks → delays + higher costs.

3. A government sets a maximum price on eggs that is lower than the market price. What happens?
A) Stores run out because producers stop supplying

B) Egg supply increases

C) Prices naturally rise anyway

D) Farmers produce more eggs out of kindness

Correct answer: A  
Teaches: price ceilings → shortages.

4. A port becomes backed up with ships waiting to unload. What is the most likely result?
A) Goods arrive late and prices rise

B) Prices fall because goods are stuck

C) Supply increases

D) Nothing changes

Correct answer: A  
Teaches: logistics bottlenecks → scarcity → higher prices.

5. If the government releases millions of barrels of oil from reserves to lower gas prices, what happens short‑term?
A) Gas prices drop because supply temporarily increases

B) Gas prices rise

C) Oil companies instantly produce more

D) Nothing changes

Correct answer: A  
Teaches: artificial supply boost → temporary price suppression.

6. What is the long‑term risk of artificially lowering prices with reserve oil releases?
A) Producers slow investment because price signals are distorted

B) Producers invest more

C) Supply becomes unlimited

D) Gas stations close permanently

Correct answer: A  
Teaches: distorted incentives → reduced future production.

7. A company raises prices because raw materials became more expensive. What is this an example of?
A) Cost‑push inflation

B) Greed

C) Random chance

D) Price gouging

Correct answer: A  
Teaches: upstream cost increases → downstream price increases.

8. A centralized authority orders all factories to produce the same amount regardless of demand. What happens?
A) Surpluses or shortages appear because signals are ignored

B) Perfect balance

C) Prices stabilize forever

D) Factories become more efficient

Correct answer: A  
Teaches: ignoring demand → misallocation.

9. A retailer notices customers buying less of a product. What is the natural free‑market response?
A) Lower the price or reduce inventory

B) Raise the price

C) Buy more of the product

D) Ignore the data

Correct answer: A  
Teaches: demand drop → price adjustment.

10. A government suddenly bans a key input used by manufacturers. What happens to the final product?
A) Prices rise and supply drops

B) Prices fall

C) Supply increases

D) Nothing changes

Correct answer: A  
Teaches: regulatory shock → scarcity → higher prices.
